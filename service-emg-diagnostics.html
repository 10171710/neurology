Advanced Peripheral Nerve and Muscle Electrodiagnostics

Electromyography (EMG) and Nerve Conduction Velocity (NCV) testing evaluate the speed and strength of electrical impulses traveling along peripheral nerves and muscles. By analyzing signal latency, conduction velocity, and recruitment patterns, our clinical neurophysiologists pinpoint the exact location and severity of nerve compressions, disc impingements, and neuromuscular diseases.

Clinical Indications & Eligibility

Radiculopathy (Pinched Nerves): Radiating shooting pain, numbness, or tingling from the neck down into the arms, or from the lower back into the legs.
Carpal Tunnel & Ulnar Entrapment: Hand numbness waking you at night, weakness dropping objects, and index/middle finger tingling.
Peripheral Neuropathy: Stocking-glove burning pain, decreased vibration sense, and balance instability in diabetic or idiopathic neuropathy.
Occipital & Cranial Neuralgia: Distinguishing localized nerve entrapment from primary vascular migraine syndromes.

The Electrodiagnostic Experience

1

Nerve Conduction Velocity (NCV) Studies

Small surface electrodes record quick, low-voltage electrical impulses traveling through sensory and motor nerves to measure conduction speed.

2

Needle Electromyography (EMG)

An ultra-thin acoustic pin sensor detects electrical activity in muscles both at rest and during mild contraction to identify denervation.

3

Same-Day Results Consultation with Neurologist

Dr. Julian Hayes reviews the waveform analysis with you before you leave, providing clear surgical or therapy recommendations.
