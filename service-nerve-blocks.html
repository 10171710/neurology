Targeted Cranial Nerve Interventions for Rapid Pain Interruption

Greater occipital nerve (GON) and sphenopalatine ganglion (SPG) blocks use targeted deposits of long-acting local anesthetics (such as bupivacaine and lidocaine) adjacent to sensory nerve bundles. This immediately desensitizes the trigeminocervical complex, breaking the cycle of intractable cranial pain without systemic sedation.

Clinical Indications & Eligibility

Acute Cluster Headache Attacks: Rapid interruption of autonomic trigeminal cycles unresponsive to oral medications.
Occipital Neuralgia: Piercing, electric-like or throbbing pain originating at the base of the skull radiating to the vertex.
Status Migrainosus Rescue: Continuous headache refractory for 72+ hours providing an immediate emergency room alternative.
Bridge Therapy: Providing swift temporary relief while initiating preventive treatments like Botox or CGRP antibodies.

The In-Office Procedure Experience

1

Palpation & Anatomical Nerve Localization

Our specialist precisely locates the occipital groove or intranasal sphenopalatine pathway.

2

Micro-Infiltration of Anesthetic (< 5 Minutes)

A small volume of bupivacaine and lidocaine is gently deposited. A mild warm tingling sensation replaces acute pain within minutes.

3

Immediate Relief & Same-Day Discharge

Patients walk out without sedation or nausea, often experiencing total relief lasting weeks to months.
