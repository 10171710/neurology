Non-Invasive Vagus Nerve Stimulation: Halting Cluster Attacks Drug-Free

How handheld gammaCore pulses delivered to the cervical vagus nerve activate inhibitory GABA pathways to interrupt trigeminal pain loops without systemic medication.

Cluster headache is among the most severe pain syndromes known to medicine, and its treatment has long depended on medications with heavy side-effect burdens. Non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation offers a different route: a drug-free, self-administered pulse of electricity that speaks directly to the brainstem's pain-control circuitry.

1. The Vagal Brake on Trigeminal Pain

The vagus nerve carries afferent fibres into the nucleus tractus solitarius, which projects to brainstem centres that modulate trigeminal nociception. Stimulating these fibres enhances inhibitory GABAergic tone, effectively applying a brake to the trigeminovascular system that generates cluster and migraine attacks.

2. gammaCore in Practice

Non-Invasive Delivery

The handheld device is placed against the neck over the cervical vagus nerve, delivering a mild, controlled electrical signal with no needles and no systemic drug load.

Cluster & Migraine Use

It can be used acutely at attack onset and preventively on a scheduled routine, making it flexible for both episodic cluster and migraine patients.

3. What the Trials Show

Randomised and open-label studies have shaped how vagus nerve stimulation is used in headache practice. Patients and clinicians should approach it as a consistent, technique-dependent therapy:

  • Reduced attack frequency when used preventively on a regular schedule.
  • Faster attack resolution when applied early at onset.
  • Favourable safety profile with predominantly local, mild side effects.
  • Best results require correct device positioning and consistent technique.
